Cole's Big Playground

The grown-up guide. About two minutes to read.

This game was built from the questionnaire you filled in. His friends are in it, his pets are in it, and his joke is in it. There is nothing to win and no score. He finds things and they go in his backpack.

You do not need to play it with him. Sitting nearby is enough.

What it practises

Every spot is a small social moment: joining in without barging, taking turns, sharing when there is not enough, having a big idea without grabbing, noticing when a body goes fast, and fixing things after. Letters, counting, adding and colours are built in underneath, at his level.

The game never tells him the lesson. It shows him a friend, gives him three things he could do, and lets him pick. One of the three is usually the cheeky choice. He can pick it twice. The third time, the game steers him to something else. Nobody is told off.

The six spots

Splash PadCatch letter blocks to spell a sign, then hold still for the tipping bucket. Holding still is the skill.
Snack ShackMatch pairs of play dough food with Amelia, taking turns. Watching her turn is the skill.
SandboxA digging race with Sebastian, then sharing the pile around the circle. Sometimes there is not enough, and the crew decides what to do.
Build ZoneBuild a clubhouse with Tristan, one piece each, or build one alone. Big ideas are welcome. Grabbing is not.
The PlaygroundRed light, green light. He walks while a friend looks away and freezes when they turn around. While he is frozen, he reads the friend's face. A different friend calls each visit.
Fix-It ShedA robot with an engine. Tap to speed it up, breathe to slow it down, keep it in the green. His body has an engine too.

Words you can use at home

The game uses a small set of words. If you use the same ones, he will recognise them.

enginefast bodyslow bodyready bodybig ideahideawayask first

Try: "Your engine is going fast. What can your body do first?" Then wait and let him answer.

If you are sitting beside him

  • Each game shows him first, then hands over. Let the showing finish before you help.
  • Say what you see, not what he should do. "You waited for Amelia." "Your body went fast there." That is enough.
  • If he picks the cheeky choice, let him. The game handles it.
  • The hideaway is a quiet fort he can go to from any game. Going there is a good choice, not quitting.
  • If it stops being fun, close the iPad. It will be there tomorrow.

Grown-up badges

He collects things in his backpack. These ones are for you. Tap the ones you have done.

Buttons and settings

  • Top right: music, voice, and Quiet. Quiet makes every sound soft.
  • Top left: the map, and the backpack with everything he has found.
  • Start over from the beginning is at the bottom of this guide. It empties the backpack, so check with him first.
  • After three visits, a spot gets a little harder. He can take the challenge or not.
  • In the Build Zone there is a camera button on the stage. It takes a photo of what he built, and the photos live behind the camera button at the top of the map. Six are kept, and each can be saved to the device or deleted.
  • Every third thing he finds, a prize machine appears. He taps to stop the spinning windows and match three in a row. It always ends in a prize, so the only surprise is which one.

Putting it on a device

The game is a website, so it works on anything with a browser. Adding it to the home screen gives it its own icon and makes it full screen. Whatever the device, open it once with wifi and after that it works without.

iPad or iPhone

  1. Open the link in Safari.
  2. Tap the share button, the square with the arrow up. On an iPhone it is at the bottom; on an iPad it is at the top.
  3. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen, then Add.

Android tablet or phone

  1. Open the link in Chrome.
  2. Tap the three dots at the top right.
  3. Tap Add to Home screen (on some phones it says Install app), then Add.

Computer

  1. Open the link in any browser and play it right there. Chrome and Edge also show a small install icon at the right end of the address bar if you want it as its own window.
  2. A touch screen is best for the games, but a mouse works: click and drag instead of sliding a finger.
